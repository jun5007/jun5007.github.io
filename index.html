ETRI Human Understanding AI Challenge

An individual competition project that became a practical lesson in the difference between a Public Leaderboard target and final generalization.

  • Public Leaderboard1st / 0.50143
  • Final Private Leaderboard118th / 0.59183

The linked public code is a PORTFOLIO_REFACTORED_VERSION; Public and Final Private results are reported separately. The test684 chain is the verified Public-best candidate, while the exact final submission selection between the remaining historical candidates has not been conclusively verified.

Approach, outcome, learning, and next steps

Initial approach

Because the Public Leaderboard ranking contributed to a course grade, the initial model-selection goal prioritized the Public score. After testing multiple models and preprocessing approaches, the entry reached Public 1st. The model and validation process were then adjusted to pursue better Private-set generalization; the Public rank moved through 3rd and 5th, and a later candidate returned to Public 1st. The exact final submission artifact cannot be independently confirmed from the surviving public files.

What worked and what did not

The Public result met the initial course objective. The Final Private result of 118th showed that the Public-stage performance did not generalize to the full test set. The remaining materials do not support a single definitive cause. Repeated Public-score checking may have indirectly guided selection toward that split, and the local validation design may not have represented the Private data well enough.

What I learned

Public rank and generalization are different criteria. Model selection should rely on a pre-defined local validation method and experiment records, not repeated reactions to leaderboard movement.

What I will change

Future competitions will track local scores by model, fold, and random seed; Public-to-local gaps; feature changes; submission rationale; probability distributions and calibration; and the final selection rule before choosing an entry.
